How the project works

Careful reading. Honest evidence. Reasoned conclusions.

A consistent approach to Bible study: respecting what Scripture means, assessing relevant evidence and remaining open to changing an interpretation when better arguments require it.

The guiding conviction

Neither faith nor evidence should have to hide.

I approach the Bible as a Christian who believes God can act supernaturally. At the same time, I won't disregard strong historical or scientific evidence just to protect a literal reading.

Nor will I turn biblical narratives into symbolism simply because they describe extraordinary events. Each passage deserves to be understood on its own terms.

The standard

“The aim is not to make every answer easy. It is to make every conclusion accountable to the evidence.”

The Bible, Investigated

How each investigation unfolds

01

The question

Identify the claim, difficulty or disagreement and why it matters to Christian belief.

02

The Scripture

Study the passage's words, genre, original languages, context and wider biblical connections.

03

The evidence

Weigh historical scholarship, archaeology, science and primary sources. Distinguish corroboration from contradiction.

04

The interpretations

Compare the strongest serious Christian explanations, including legitimate disagreements across traditions.

05

The conclusion

Give the most defensible provisional judgment, explain confidence and identify unresolved problems.

06

Revisit when necessary

Correct errors and revise claims when new research or better arguments justify doing so.

Editorial promises

What I will and won't do

Studies begin with the natural intended meaning of the biblical passage. Established evidence is taken seriously; lack of outside corroboration is not automatically treated as disproof. Miracles are considered possible without using them as convenient explanations for every contradiction.

When Scripture appears internally inconsistent—or appears to conflict with reliable external evidence—the investigation will test real explanations and say clearly when none is satisfactory. The aim is to evaluate competing interpretations, not just list them.

The public project is non-denominational. Where traditions differ, relevant views may be compared fairly, but no church receives special status. The published articles do not reproduce private personal denominational assessments.

Publication safeguard

Detailed theological judgments are only published after the underlying private study has been completed and specifically approved for public release. The website does not represent unstudied passages as settled.

Sources and corrections

Substantial studies will identify the evidence they rely upon, distinguish confident findings from disputed claims, and record consequential corrections clearly. A publication date and relevant references will accompany each completed investigation.
